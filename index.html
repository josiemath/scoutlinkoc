ScoutLink OC is a proposed council-wide platform for Girl Scouts of Orange County. Leaders find partner troops in other service units, events reach girls beyond their own troop, and girls plan their own projects from proposal to wrap-up.

Built for the space between troops

Girls and leaders usually know what's happening inside their own troop. Visibility drops off at the edge of it: the beach cleanup two cities over, the Juniors looking for robotics teammates, the Ambassadors offering Gold Award mentoring.

The Volunteer Toolkit already handles meeting plans, troop calendars, attendance and badges inside a troop. ScoutLink OC leaves those alone and focuses on what happens between troops and service units: finding each other, sharing events, and running projects together.

What it does

Five tools, one account. Think networking, troop-to-troop messaging, event registration and project boards, built around how Girl Scouts already organize.

Try them in the demo

  • Troop and service unit directory

    Browse by service unit, then troop, then troop profile. Filter by level, interests, size and years active. Leaders can request a collaboration with any troop, not only the ones in their own service unit.

    Browse the directory

  • Leader-to-leader messaging

    Verified adults message each other to set up partnerships and share resources. Girls talk in project spaces that troop adults moderate, never in private chats with adults.

    See leader messages

  • Shared calendar and registration

    Troops publish events for their troop, their service unit or the whole council. Registration handles capacity, waitlists, level eligibility and caregiver permission. A girl can propose an event and send it to her leader for review.

    Find an event

  • Girl-led project workspaces

    Task boards, budgets, milestones and files for troop initiatives and Bronze, Silver and Gold Award projects. Girls can export a progress summary; official award approval still goes through the council.

    Open a sample project

  • Opportunity board

    Requests for volunteers, joint project ideas, mentorship offers and gear to lend, from troops across the council.

    Read the board

Everyone sees what they need, and nothing more

The platform serves minors, so what you can do depends on who you are. The demo lets you switch between all six roles to see the difference.

Safety built in

  • Verified accounts for every adult
  • No direct messaging between adults and girls
  • Caregiver permission for events and for every account under 13
  • Exact meeting points shared only with registered troops
  • Member lists, private meetings and travel plans stay inside the troop
  • GSOC reviews the implementation against its current policies before launch

How it fits with existing tools

ScoutLink OC shouldn't replace what already works. The difference is networking between troops plus girl-led project management, rather than ordinary group messaging.

Roadmap

Start small, test with a few troops, then grow. These are planning estimates for a focused development effort, not launch dates. Council approvals, sign-in setup and privacy testing could change the schedule.

  1. 1

    Minimum viable product

    About 6–10 weeks

    Verified adult accounts, searchable troop and service unit profiles, leader contact requests, shareable event calendars and basic registration. Tested with a small group of troops.

  2. 2

    Girl-led project tools

    About 4–6 more weeks

    Approved youth accounts, task boards, milestones, budgets, document sharing and event proposal workflows.

  3. 3

    Council-wide community

    Depends on GSOC participation

    More service units, the opportunity board, moderated collaboration spaces, notifications and administrator reporting.

Connecting troops. Empowering girls. Building community.

ScoutLink OC is a proposed digital collaboration platform designed to strengthen communication and community engagement across Girl Scouts of Orange County.

By connecting troop leaders, volunteers and Girl Scouts across different service units, the platform would make it easier to discover opportunities, organize joint activities and build meaningful partnerships.

Its event registration and girl-led project tools would let Girl Scouts take ownership of their initiatives while giving volunteers the oversight and organization they need.

The vision is a more connected Girl Scout community, where collaboration extends beyond individual troops and every girl has more chances to lead.

The ask

Start as a GSOC-supported pilot with a few troops rather than council-wide adoption, so leaders, coordinators and girls can give feedback before the more complex features are built. As part of the pilot, GSOC would decide which non-sensitive troop directory information can be shared with verified volunteers.
